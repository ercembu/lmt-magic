Sealed is mostly one decision: pick two colours, then play the best 23 cards in them. The part of that decision this tool is actually good at is which individual cards are worth committing for. The colour and pair rankings below were tested against 21 released sets and came out weak — each section says how weak, and you should read them in that order.

What the model thinks of each colour

Tested, and it is weak. Across 31 released sets this ordering matched the real one at a rank correlation of 0.22. It named the actual deepest colour 9 times in 31 and the actual shallowest 8 times in 31, against 6 by chance. The reason it stays poor is measurable: the real gap between colours in a set is about 0.20, and our error on a colour's score is about 0.18 — the difference we are trying to see is nearly the size of our mistake. Worth a glance, not worth a decision.

Cards that decide a colour on their own

The most reliable thing here. These come straight from the card grades, and the model is at its best on a set's top cards — on held-out sets its top 10 landed at the 91st–95th percentile of what actually happened. Single-coloured cards only. A mono-coloured bomb commits one colour and leaves the other open, which is exactly what you want from your first pack. Gold cards commit both at once, so they live further down with their pairs.
